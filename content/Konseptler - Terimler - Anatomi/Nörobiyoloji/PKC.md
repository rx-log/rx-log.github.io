
First of all, patients who are manic or hypomanic have overactivated PKC activity. Also as we all know; PKC inhibitors exerts anti-manic properties. 
Piracetam stimulates the synthesis of phosphatidylinositol, increases the release of IP3 and the breakdown of PIP2(and by hydrolysis of PIP2, DAG and IP3 is also increased)[X](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/482348/).
On top of that, piracetam exerts a "priming" effect on IP3 release[X](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9890564/).
Adding Inositol to our regimen increases PIP2/PIP2 pool and increases the consumption of the precursors of PI(phosphatidylinositol, which again, increases PIP2 synthesis via phosphorylation by PI4K to PI4P, and then by PIP5K to PIP2)[X](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2075267/). Increased concentrations of PIP2 subsequently increases DAG and IP3. DAG signalling(also PIP2, but to a lesser extent and context-dependent), based on duration and magnitude, stimulates/activates PKC.

Also:
Activated PLC(phospholipase C) generates IP3 and DAG from PIP2. IP3 and DAG trigger Ca2+ release through IP3 receptors. DAG remains in the membrane and co-activates PKC with Ca2+(but ezra says piracetam interacts with novel pkc aka npkc, which doesn't require Ca2+ to activate pkc).
